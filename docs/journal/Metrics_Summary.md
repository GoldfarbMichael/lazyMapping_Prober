# Metrics Summary — Coverage, Cleansing, Spillover

Generated by `stable/python/metrics_summary.py`. Definitions: `Metrics_Definitions.md`.
`--` = not collected at that NoC.

## Conventions

| metric | range | direction | aggregation over samples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coverage | [0,1] | higher better | **min** (worst-case sample) |
| Cleansing | [0,1] | higher better | **min** (worst-case sample) |
| Spillover | [0,1] | **lower** better | **mean** |

**The aggregation is deliberately not uniform.** Coverage and cleansing keep the established
worst-case `min`; spillover uses the **mean** (project decision — its `max` is very noisy at n=2).
This is stated here so the asymmetry is explicit rather than hidden in the numbers.

**Reading them together.** Spillover is degenerate on its own — a victim that evicts nothing scores
a perfect 0. It is the false-positive rate to coverage's true-positive rate; a spillover figure
without its coverage figure is meaningless.

**Sample counts (important for cross-row comparison).** Every tree has **n = 2** except
`bidirR4 0x0`, which has **n = 10** (n = 6 at NoC 2). A `min` over 10 draws is systematically lower
than a `min` over 2, so `bidirR4 0x0` is **penalised** relative to the other rows on coverage and
cleansing — its true worst case is better than the table shows relative to its neighbours.
Spillover is unaffected, since the mean is unbiased in n. Exact counts are in the long-form table.
